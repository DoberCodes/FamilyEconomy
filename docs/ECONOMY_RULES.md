# Economy Rules

Family Economy models a fictional household economy for education. These rules are product rules first; implementation should follow them unless a future product decision changes the direction.

## Fictional Credits

- Credits are fictional educational units.
- Credits are not cash, deposits, stored value, or payment instruments.
- Balances are learning feedback, not financial accounts.
- Statements are educational records, not bank statements.

## Parent Authority

Parents control:

- Jobs
- Rewards
- Savings approvals
- Reward approvals
- Tax policies
- Family fund/community spending decisions
- Allowance settings, if added
- Child session access

Children participate within the parent-defined household framework.

## Earning

Children can earn fictional credits or XP through parent-defined jobs and responsibilities.

Guidelines:

- Credits should usually represent meaningful contribution.
- XP can recognize responsibility without inflating spendable credits.
- Parent review should be available where trust, age, or job type calls for it.
- Missed-job and failed-check consequences should be explainable and proportionate.

## Saving And Goals

Goals are a primary learning mechanism.

Guidelines:

- Make goals visible and understandable.
- Show progress clearly.
- Encourage children to compare saving vs spending choices.
- Support both personal and family goals.
- Use parent approval/counter offers to teach negotiation and planning.

## Spending And Rewards

Rewards teach budgeting and decision making.

Guidelines:

- Reward costs use fictional credits only.
- Rewards should be parent-defined or parent-approved.
- Reward requests may be approved, denied, countered, fulfilled, or redirected to the family pool.
- Avoid randomized rewards, loot boxes, gambling-like mechanics, or surprise costs.

## Family Tax

Family tax is planned as an optional educational tool.

Purpose:

- Teach shared responsibility.
- Demonstrate how taxes fund community benefits.
- Create visible links between contributions and family-wide outcomes.

Guidelines:

- Taxes should not be used as punishment.
- Parents should control whether tax is enabled and what rate applies.
- Children should see where taxed credits go and what the family fund supports.
- Copy should frame tax as contribution, not confiscation.

## Family Fund And Community Projects

The family fund is a planned shared pool for community-oriented spending.

Examples:

- Family movie night
- Shared game or activity
- Zoo trip
- Garden project
- Camping trip

Guidelines:

- Show the group benefit.
- Support family voting or parent-guided decisions.
- Encourage collaborative planning.
- Keep the fund fictional and educational.

## Allowance

Allowance may be introduced later as an optional parent-controlled concept.

Guidelines:

- Keep in-app credits educational-only.
- Do not imply automatic real-world money movement.
- If families connect credits to offline allowance practices, frame that as a parent-managed household choice outside the app.

## Gamification Limits

Allowed when educational and parent-aligned:

- XP for responsibility
- Lightweight badges
- Progress bars
- Gentle celebrations
- Goal milestones
- Activity summaries

Avoid:

- Loot boxes
- Gambling systems
- Randomized rewards
- Manipulative streak pressure
- Artificial scarcity
- Engagement-first loops
- Fear-of-missing-out mechanics

## Dynamic Pricing And Scarcity

Dynamic pricing, limits, and scarcity concepts should teach tradeoffs, planning, and supply/demand.

Guidelines:

- Parent controls must remain clear.
- Children should be able to understand why a cost changed.
- Avoid artificial pressure or surprise changes.
- Prefer explainable educational framing over engagement tactics.

## Business Rule Priorities

When rules conflict, use this order:

1. Child safety and parent authority
2. Fictional-credit clarity
3. Educational value
4. Goal-based learning
5. Community responsibility
6. Simplicity and usability
7. Entertainment value
