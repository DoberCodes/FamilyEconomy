# Economy Rules

This document defines the behavioral, educational, and design rules that govern the Family Economy system.

Product purpose, educational philosophy, and platform guardrails are defined in `VISION.md`.

If there is a conflict between this document and `VISION.md`, `VISION.md` is authoritative.

---

# Purpose

Family Economy exists to teach financial literacy through a fictional household economy.

The economy should help families teach:

* Responsibility
* Saving
* Spending
* Goal setting
* Delayed gratification
* Community contribution
* Budgeting
* Financial planning
* Decision-making

The economy exists to support learning and family discussion.

It does not exist to maximize engagement or screen time.

---

# Foundational Rules

## Educational First

Every economy feature should answer:

> What educational outcome does this teach?

Features that do not clearly support an educational outcome should be reconsidered.

---

## Fictional Credits Only

Credits are fictional educational units.

Credits are not:

* Currency
* Money
* Stored value
* Financial assets
* Investments
* Real-world balances

Credits exist only to teach concepts.

---

## Parent Authority

Parents remain the governing authority of the economy.

Parents control:

* Jobs
* Rewards
* Goals
* Community funds
* Approval workflows
* Recognition settings
* Advanced economy features

Children participate within parent-defined boundaries.

---

## Family First

The economy should encourage:

* Family participation
* Shared goals
* Family discussion
* Community contribution
* Collaborative decision-making

The economy should not encourage isolation or purely individual outcomes.

---

# Credit Rules

## Credits Are Earned

Credits should primarily enter the economy through participation.

Examples:

* Jobs
* Responsibilities
* Family contributions
* Positive behaviors

Credits should generally not be granted randomly.

---

## Credits Have Purpose

Credits should support:

* Rewards
* Saving
* Goals
* Community contribution

Credits should encourage intentional decision-making.

---

## Avoid Wealth-Based Status

The economy should avoid rewarding children simply for having the highest balance.

Avoid recognition categories such as:

* Richest Child
* Largest Wallet
* Most Wealth

Prefer categories that reinforce positive behaviors.

---

# Goal Rules

## Goals Are Educational

Goals exist to teach:

* Delayed gratification
* Planning
* Saving
* Long-term thinking

Goals should remain one of the primary educational mechanisms in the system.

---

## Goal Progress Should Be Visible

Children should be able to clearly see:

* Target amount
* Current progress
* Remaining amount
* Completion status

Progress should feel motivating but not manipulative.

---

## Family Goals Matter

Family goals should reinforce:

* Collaboration
* Shared responsibility
* Group achievement

Family goals should be prominently visible within the family experience.

---

# Recognition Rules

Recognition exists to celebrate participation, contribution, and growth.

Recognition should never become a wealth competition.

---

## Recognition Should Reward

* Effort
* Consistency
* Helping others
* Goal completion
* Community contribution
* Saving habits
* Positive participation

---

## Recognition Should Avoid

* Wealth accumulation
* Financial status
* Punishment
* Public embarrassment
* Excessive competition

---

## Weekly Recognition Categories

Examples include:

* Hardest Worker
* Most Helpful
* Goal Setter
* Most Generous
* Consistency Champion
* Rising Star
* Top Saver

Additional categories should align with educational outcomes.

---

## Recognition Philosophy

Recognition should feel like:

```text
Family Awards
```

not:

```text
Financial Rankings
```

The goal is celebration and encouragement.

---

# Community Rules

Community systems teach that individual actions affect larger groups.

---

## Contributions

Contributions should teach:

* Shared responsibility
* Community participation
* Collective benefit

Contributions should not feel punitive.

---

## Community Funds

Community funds should be visibly connected to positive family outcomes.

Examples:

* Family outings
* Shared rewards
* Family projects
* Community goals

Children should understand how contributions create shared benefits.

---

## Family Projects

Family projects should encourage:

* Teamwork
* Collaboration
* Shared ownership
* Collective success

---

# Scarcity Rules

Scarcity should only exist when it teaches something useful.

---

## Acceptable Scarcity

Examples:

* Limited family resources
* Tradeoffs between goals
* Shared resource decisions

These concepts teach planning and prioritization.

---

## Unacceptable Scarcity

Avoid:

* Artificial waiting
* Pay-to-progress mechanics
* Engagement-driven limitations
* Retention loops

Scarcity should never exist solely to increase app usage.

---

# Competition Rules

Competition is optional.

Competition should encourage participation rather than create winners and losers.

---

## Healthy Competition

Examples:

* Weekly recognition
* Friendly family challenges
* Goal-based competitions

---

## Unhealthy Competition

Avoid:

* Public shaming
* Permanent rankings
* Wealth comparisons
* Systems that consistently favor older children

Recognition systems should allow all children opportunities to succeed.

---

# Advanced Economy Rules

Advanced Economy features should be educational simulations only.

Advanced Economy is intended primarily for older children.

---

## Savings Accounts

Savings Accounts teach:

* Saving habits
* Financial planning
* Emergency preparedness

Savings Accounts remain fictional educational systems.

---

## Expenses

Expenses teach:

* Budgeting
* Cash flow
* Planning
* Financial responsibility

Expenses should be framed as educational household contributions rather than real-world bills.

Examples:

* Housing Contribution
* Food Contribution
* Utilities Contribution
* Transportation Contribution

---

## Family Bank

Family Bank systems teach:

* Lending
* Borrowing
* Repayment
* Financial planning

All lending remains fictional and educational.

---

## Credit Concepts

Credit simulations should teach:

* Trust
* Repayment
* Financial responsibility

No real-world credit reporting exists.

---

## Investing Concepts

Investing simulations should teach:

* Risk
* Reward
* Long-term thinking

No real-world investing occurs.

---

## Family Business Concepts

Family Business systems should teach:

* Entrepreneurship
* Planning
* Resource management
* Financial decision-making

Business systems remain educational simulations.

---

# Gamification Rules

Gamification should support learning outcomes.

Gamification should never become the purpose of the product.

---

## Acceptable Gamification

* XP
* Levels
* Achievements
* Badges
* Recognition
* Progress tracking

---

## Unacceptable Gamification

* Loot boxes
* Randomized rewards
* Gambling mechanics
* Excessive streak pressure
* Artificial urgency
* Fear-of-missing-out mechanics

---

# Success Criteria

A feature aligns with Family Economy if it helps families:

* Learn together
* Save together
* Plan together
* Contribute together
* Celebrate together

The best Family Economy features create meaningful conversations between parents and children while reinforcing healthy financial habits in a safe, fictional environment.
