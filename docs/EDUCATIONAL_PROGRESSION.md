# Educational Progression

This document defines how Family Economy concepts should be introduced as children mature.

Product purpose, philosophy, and educational guardrails are defined in `VISION.md`.

If there is a conflict between this document and `VISION.md`, `VISION.md` is authoritative.

---

# Purpose

Family Economy should grow with the child.

The goal is not to expose every financial concept immediately.

Instead, the platform should introduce concepts gradually as children develop the maturity and experience to understand them.

Each stage should build naturally upon concepts introduced in earlier stages.

---

# Progression Principles

## Start Simple

Children should first understand:

* Earning
* Saving
* Spending
* Sharing

before introducing more advanced concepts.

---

## Build On Existing Knowledge

New concepts should extend previously learned concepts.

Example progression:

```text
Earn
↓
Save
↓
Goal Setting
↓
Budgeting
↓
Emergency Funds
↓
Loans
↓
Investing
```

---

## Complexity Should Be Optional

Parents should be able to decide when advanced concepts are introduced.

Not every family will enable every economy feature.

---

## Educational Before Realistic

The goal is understanding.

The system should not attempt to perfectly simulate the real world.

Concepts should be simplified when appropriate to improve learning.

# Stage 1: Foundations

Recommended Ages: 4–8

Focus:

```text
Earn
Save
Spend
Share
```

---

## Concepts

### Jobs

Children learn:

* Responsibility
* Effort
* Contribution

---

### Rewards

Children learn:

* Spending decisions
* Tradeoffs
* Value

---

### Savings Goals

Children learn:

* Delayed gratification
* Planning
* Patience

---

### Family Contributions

Children learn:

* Sharing
* Helping others
* Community participation

---

### Recognition

Children learn:

* Positive reinforcement
* Celebration
* Participation

Recognition should focus on effort and contribution.

---

Focus:

```text
Recognition
Community
Planning
```

---

## Concepts

### Family Goals

Children learn:

* Collaboration
* Shared ownership
* Group achievement

---

### Community Funds

Children learn:

* Shared responsibility
* Collective benefit
* Resource allocation

---

### Recognition Systems

Children learn:

* Consistency
* Growth
* Positive participation

Examples:

* Hardest Worker
* Most Helpful
* Goal Setter
* Most Generous
* Consistency Champion

---

### Family Projects

Children learn:

* Teamwork
* Shared objectives
* Long-term planning

Children begin learning:

* Limited resources

# Stage 3: Financial Planning

Recommended Ages: 12–15

Focus:

```text
Budgeting
Planning
Saving
Responsibility
```

---

## Concepts

### Savings Accounts

Children learn:

* Saving without immediate spending
* Future planning
* Emergency preparation

---

### Emergency Funds

Children learn:

* Financial resilience
* Unexpected expenses
* Long-term thinking

---

### Expenses

Optional fictional expenses teach:

* Budgeting
* Cash flow
* Financial planning

Examples:

* Housing Contribution
* Food Contribution
* Utilities Contribution
* Transportation Contribution

Expenses should always remain educational simulations.

---

### Financial Tradeoffs

Children learn:

* Opportunity cost
* Budget allocation
* Prioritization

---

# Stage 4: Advanced Financial Concepts

Recommended Ages: 15+

Focus:

```text
Credit
Investing
Entrepreneurship
Planning
```

---

## Concepts

### Family Bank

Children learn:

* Lending
* Borrowing
* Repayment

---

### Credit Simulation

Children learn:

* Trust
* Financial responsibility
* Long-term consequences

No real-world credit activity exists.

---

### Investing Simulation

Children learn:

* Risk
* Reward
* Long-term growth

All investing remains fictional.

---

### Entrepreneurship

Children learn:

* Business ownership
* Resource management
* Planning
* Risk assessment

---

### Family Business

Potential concepts:

* Equipment ownership
* Equipment rental
* Business expenses
* Business income

All business systems remain educational simulations.

# Recognition Progression

Recognition should evolve alongside the child.

---

## Early Recognition

Focus:

* Participation
* Effort
* Helping

Examples:

* Family Helper
* Goal Starter
* Team Player

---

## Intermediate Recognition

Focus:

* Consistency
* Growth
* Contribution

Examples:

* Hardest Worker
* Most Helpful
* Most Generous
* Consistency Champion

---

## Advanced Recognition

Focus:

* Planning
* Saving
* Financial decision-making

Examples:

* Top Saver
* Budget Master
* Prepared Planner
* Community Builder

Recognition should never become a wealth-ranking system.

---

# Parent Guidance

Parents should be able to choose how quickly concepts are introduced.

Recommended approach:

1. Start with Jobs, Rewards, and Goals.
2. Introduce Family Goals and Community Funds.
3. Introduce Savings Accounts and Expenses.
4. Introduce Family Bank and Investing concepts.
5. Introduce Entrepreneurship and Family Business systems.

Families should be able to stop at any stage and still receive meaningful educational value.

---

# Success Criteria

A child progressing through Family Economy should gradually develop understanding of:

* Responsibility
* Saving
* Spending
* Goal Setting
* Delayed Gratification
* Community Contribution
* Budgeting
* Financial Planning
* Credit Concepts
* Investing Concepts
* Entrepreneurship

The platform should feel like a natural progression from helping around the house to understanding real-world financial concepts while remaining safe, fictional, and parent-controlled.
